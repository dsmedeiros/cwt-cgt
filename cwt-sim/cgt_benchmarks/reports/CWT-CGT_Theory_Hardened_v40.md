# CWT-CGT Theory — Hardened v40

## Core status
The theory remains strongest as a **branch-resolved geometric-response framework** with clearly separated lanes:
- coherent/passive branch geometry,
- explicit branch-switching / R4 exclusion,
- auxiliary periodic/gapped topology,
- noisy open-system scaffold response.

The main new result in v40 is that the noisy loop-side rule is no longer supported only by benchmark C and a one-way transfer to benchmark G. A **single pooled scaffold rule** can now be derived from the square/circle train rows of **both positive noisy scaffold benchmarks C and G**, and then evaluated on held-out loop families for both benchmarks with **no benchmark-specific refit**.

## Accepted noisy pooled scaffold rule
At each dephasing level, pool the train rows from benchmarks C and G, derive one Phase-39-style compactness-normalizer rule, and evaluate on held-out families of both benchmarks.

This remains a scaffold-only result, but it is stronger than either:
1. a one-benchmark noisy success, or
2. a one-way C-to-G transfer only.

## Switch-slice result (`γ = 0.30`)
Under the pooled scaffold rule:
- benchmark C held-out combined: `R² ≈ 0.9412`
- benchmark G held-out combined: `R² ≈ 0.9277`
- pooled held-out combined across both benchmarks: `R² ≈ 0.9380`
- pooled held-out new-family across both benchmarks: `R² ≈ 0.9191`
- pooled held-out combined correlation: `≈ 0.9788`
- sign agreement: `1.0`

## Interpretation
This supports a stronger statement than before:

> Inside the current designed noisy scaffold family, there is evidence for a **shared positive noisy geometric-response rule**, not merely two unrelated benchmark-specific successes.

## Boundary
This is still **not** a complete or external validation.
The pooled rule is derived from scaffold train rows and tested on scaffold held-out families. It improves confidence in the noisy extension **inside the scaffold program**, but it does not yet make the theory broadly universal.
